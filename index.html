AT THE MOVIES

The Shawshank Redemption: Hope Is Never Locked Up

Romans 5:3-5 Acts 16:22-34 Isaiah 43:2

Big Idea

Hope is not found in perfect circumstances. Hope is produced by God in the middle of what we are going through.

“Suffering produces perseverance; perseverance, character; and character, hope.”
Romans 5:3-4
Opening Question:

Where in my life do I need to believe that hope is still alive?

1

God Produces Hope Through What You’re Going Through

Romans 5:3-5

“And hope does not put us to shame, because God’s love has been poured out into our hearts through the Holy Spirit.”
Romans 5:5

Paul reminds us that suffering is not the end of the story. God can use what we are going through to produce perseverance, character, and hope.

Hope is not denial. Hope is not pretending everything is fine. Biblical hope is confidence that God is still present, still working, and still faithful.

God does not waste what He can use.

2

Your Prison Does Not Have The Final Word

Acts 16:22-34

“About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the other prisoners were listening to them.”
Acts 16:25

Paul and Silas were beaten, chained, and locked in prison. But even there, their worship was not locked up.

Their bodies were confined, but their hope was free. Their circumstances were painful, but God was still moving.

People can lock doors, but they cannot lock up the hope of Christ.

3

God’s Presence Is Greater Than Your Prison

Isaiah 43:2

“When you pass through the waters, I will be with you.”
Isaiah 43:2

God does not promise that we will never walk through deep waters, rivers, or fire. He promises that when we walk through them, we will not walk alone.

The prison may be real. The pain may be real. The waiting may be real. But God’s presence is greater.

God may not remove every prison immediately, but He promises to be present in it faithfully.

Remember This

  • You are not forgotten.
  • You are not abandoned.
  • You are not beyond God’s reach.
  • You are not without hope.
