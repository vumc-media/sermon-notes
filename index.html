Acts: The Church That Changed the World

What the Enemy Meant for Evil

Acts 8:1–25

When disruption becomes mission

The church in Jerusalem had experienced incredible growth, but everything changed when persecution broke out. Believers were scattered throughout Judea and Samaria.

What looked like the destruction of the church became the movement that carried the good news beyond Jerusalem. The enemy intended scattering, fear and silence—but God was still at work.

Sermon Point 1

What scatters you doesn’t have to silence you.

Acts 8:4

The believers were scattered, but they did not stop sharing the good news. Their circumstances changed. Their mission did not.

Consider this: Being displaced does not mean being disqualified. God can still use your voice wherever life has placed you.

Reflect

What disruption, disappointment or unexpected change has made you tempted to become quiet, withdraw or give up?

Sermon Point 2

God can turn your disruption into somebody else’s destination.

Philip did not choose the circumstances that sent him toward Samaria, but God used his journey to bring the good news of Jesus to people who needed to hear it.

What looked like interruption became opportunity. What looked like scattering became sending. And a place the disciples might never have reached otherwise became a place of transformation and joy.

Consider this: Sometimes God does His greatest work through roads we never planned to travel.

Reflect

Who might God be positioning you to encourage, serve or reach because of something unexpected happening in your life?

Take It With You

The early church discovered that opposition could scatter believers, but it could not stop the gospel.

This week, instead of asking only, “Why is this happening to me?” consider asking, “Where might God be sending me through this?”
