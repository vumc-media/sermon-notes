Into the Wilderness

The Crown and the Cross

Matthew 27:27–54

Main Scripture

Matthew 27:27–54

Jesus is mocked, crowned with thorns, nailed to the cross, and revealed as King not through worldly power, but through suffering love and saving sacrifice.

Sermon Focus

Big Idea: The crown of thorns and the cross both reveal who Jesus truly is: the King who takes our curse and the Savior who shows the power of God through sacrificial love.

Key Truth: What looked like humiliation was actually holy revelation.

Point 1

The Crown of Thorns Reveals the True King
Supporting Scripture: Genesis 3:18

Thorns first appeared in Scripture as part of the curse after sin entered the world. When Jesus wore the crown of thorns, He was not only being mocked by soldiers. He was taking upon Himself the visible symbol of humanity’s brokenness.

  • The thorns point back to the fall.
  • Jesus wears what sin produced.
  • The true King steps into our pain instead of standing far away from it.
  • He does not rule by cruelty, but by sacrifice.

Preaching line: Jesus wears the curse so we can receive grace.

What stands out to you from Point 1?

Point 2

The Cross Reveals the Power of God
Supporting Scripture: Matthew 27:37

The sign above Jesus’ head was meant to mock Him: “This is Jesus, the King of the Jews.” But the cross did not expose weakness. It revealed the unexpected power of God.

  • Human power takes. God’s power gives.
  • Human power protects itself. God’s power pours itself out.
  • The cross looked like defeat, but heaven declared victory.
  • In Jesus, suffering becomes the place where redemption breaks through.

Preaching line: The power of God is often seen most clearly where the world only sees weakness.

What stands out to you from Point 2?

Scripture Reflection

What does this passage show you about Jesus?
